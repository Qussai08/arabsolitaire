# Narrative Canon & Story Bible
## سوليتير العرب: أسطورة المعاني

**Version:** 1.1  
**Status:** FINAL APPROVED NARRATIVE CANON BASELINE  
**Scope:** Core World, Characters, Villain, Arc 1, Chapter Story Beats, Presentation Rules, Narrative Guardrails

---

# 1. Official Game Title

## **سوليتير العرب: أسطورة المعاني**

The title reflects the player’s eventual narrative identity.

The player does not begin the story already known as “أسطورة المعاني”.

The title is earned through the first major story arc and is formally bestowed at the end of Chapter 5.

---

# 2. Official Narrative Thesis

> **اللغة لا تعيش بالكلمات وحدها؛ تعيش بالمعاني والروابط التي نصنعها بينها.**

This is the central narrative statement of the game.

Everything in the world, gameplay, progression, and conflict should reinforce this idea.

---

# 3. Dar Al-Rawabit

## **دار الروابط**

Dar Al-Rawabit is the living memory of the Arabic language.

It preserves not only words, but the relationships between:

- words
- meanings
- ideas
- places
- symbols
- cultural references
- memories
- expressions
- concepts
- patterns
- knowledge

Every meaningful relationship used, remembered, or created by people leaves an echo inside Dar Al-Rawabit.

When relationships are weakened or forgotten, the corresponding parts of Dar Al-Rawabit weaken as well.

Dar Al-Rawabit is not a single building.

It is a vast interconnected network with manifestations across different cities and regions.

Each city contains a local Node/branch of the same greater entity.

---

# 4. The Threat to Arabic

The Arabic language is under deliberate attack.

The objective of the antagonist is not merely to destroy written words.

His goal is more dangerous:

## To destroy the relationships that give Arabic its richness, depth, memory, and meaning.

If enough meanings are separated from their relationships, the language can remain visibly present while becoming hollow.

This is the narrative equivalent of the core Association gameplay.

---

# 5. The Distortion

## **التشويش**

The Distortion is a conscious/intelligent force.

It emerged from forgotten, abandoned, weakened, or disconnected meanings and relationships.

It was not fully created by the main villain.

Instead:

- it existed as a dormant force;
- the villain discovered it;
- he awakened it;
- he learned to direct it;
- he uses it as the primary weapon against Dar Al-Rawabit.

The Distortion can:

- cut relationships;
- scramble meaning;
- create false relationships;
- corrupt semantic connections;
- reshape parts of Dar Al-Rawabit;
- react to the player;
- spread between Nodes.

It behaves as an intelligent force rather than random visual corruption.

---

# 6. Main Villain — APPROVED CANON

The main antagonist is a powerful evil sorcerer.

He has two canonical titles with different origins and meanings.

## **عدو العرب — Enemy of the Arabs**

This is the name/title he chose for himself.

He deliberately adopts it as a declaration of hatred and identity.

He wants the title to communicate that his conflict is not accidental or temporary.

It is the name he wants others to fear.

## **المُبدِّد — The Dissipator / The Eraser**

This is the name given to him by the Arabs.

They call him المُبدِّد because he:

- scatters meanings;
- breaks relationships;
- separates words from what gives them depth;
- fragments cultural memory;
- spreads The Distortion through Dar Al-Rawabit.

The distinction is important:

- **عدو العرب** = the identity he claims for himself.
- **المُبدِّد** = the name the Arabs give him based on what he does.

Throughout the story, characters may use either title depending on perspective.

Shiboub and the Guardians are more likely to call him:

## **المُبدِّد**

The villain himself is more likely to proclaim:

## **عدو العرب**

He came from distant lands to the Arab world.

The Arabs welcomed him with generosity and hospitality.

He was given:

- shelter;
- food;
- trust;
- safety.

Their hospitality did not heal his resentment.

His envy and hatred had already taken root.

# 7. Villain Motivation — APPROVED CANON

The villain’s hatred is rooted in:

## **فقره اللغوي أمام ثراء العربية — his own linguistic poverty when confronted with the richness of Arabic**

He comes from a background in which his own linguistic world feels narrow, limited, and poor compared with what he encounters in Arabic.

When he enters the Arab world, he is confronted by:

- the vast vocabulary of Arabic;
- multiple shades of meaning;
- layered expressions;
- poetry and rhetoric;
- cultural memory carried through language;
- the ability of one concept to connect to many meanings;
- the depth and variety of Arab cultural expression.

Instead of responding with curiosity or admiration, he experiences this richness as humiliation.

His linguistic insecurity becomes:

- envy;
- resentment;
- wounded pride;
- then hatred.

He comes to believe that what he lacks must be destroyed.

Therefore, his war against Arabic is deeply personal.

He does not simply hate Arabs for existing.

He hates what their language and culture represent to him:

## **a richness he feels incapable of possessing.**

This envy becomes the emotional foundation of his plan to break meanings, scatter relationships, and reduce Arabic to disconnected fragments.

His strategy is therefore symbolically consistent:

> He cannot equal the richness of the language, so he tries to impoverish the language itself.

---

# 8. Relationship Between the Villain and The Distortion

عدو العرب did not create The Distortion from nothing.

He discovered and awakened it.

He learned how to:

- feed it;
- direct it;
- move it between Nodes;
- use forgotten meanings as fuel;
- manipulate its intelligent behavior.

The Distortion is therefore both:

- a weapon under his influence;
- and a dangerous force with its own behavior.

This leaves room for later story arcs to reveal that even the villain may not fully control what he unleashed.

---

# 9. Player Ability

The player possesses a rare ability called:

## **بصيرة المعنى — Insight of Meaning**

This allows the player to perceive damaged relationships that others cannot see.

The player can:

- see hidden semantic threads;
- recognize broken connections;
- restore correct relationships;
- resist false relationships;
- stabilize damaged Nodes;
- perceive pathways through The Distortion.

This power directly explains why the player can perform the core Association gameplay.

---

# 10. Player Identity

The player begins without the title “أسطورة المعاني”.

The journey is about earning that title.

Across Arc 1, the player proves that they can:

- restore damaged meaning;
- survive increasingly intelligent Distortion;
- repair major Nodes;
- continue despite the scale of the threat.

At the end of Chapter 5, Shiboub formally calls the player:

## **يا أسطورة المعاني**

This is the first canonical use of the title inside the story.

---

# 11. Shiboub

## **شيبوب**

Shiboub is the player’s recurring companion.

He is the last remaining guide from the old generation of Dar Al-Rawabit guardians.

He understands:

- the old rules of the archive;
- many hidden paths;
- the history of the Nodes;
- old mechanisms inside Dar Al-Rawabit.

However, he does not know every truth.

He also hides important information because of guilt.

---

# 12. Shiboub Visual Canon

Shiboub is:

- a short-statured man;
- dark-skinned;
- thick curly black hair;
- highly expressive;
- visually recognizable in silhouette;
- inspired by Arabic historical/cultural aesthetics;
- designed as an original fantasy/adventure character.

His design may include:

- a small bag containing cards, keys, notes, and strange archive objects;
- a short staff or oversized pen-like tool used for pointing and interacting with the archive;
- layered garments inspired by Arabic clothing without being a literal historical costume.

His design must remain original.

It should not directly copy any existing visual depiction of the historical/literary figure.

---

# 13. Shiboub Visual Style

Approved style:

## **Stylized 3D / painterly cartoon**

The target feeling is:

- animated adventure;
- expressive;
- cinematic but accessible;
- not realistic;
- not chibi;
- suitable for humor and emotional story moments.

---

# 14. Shiboub Personality

Shiboub is clearly comedic.

His humor is a major part of his personality.

He uses:

- sarcasm;
- exaggeration;
- expressive reactions;
- light colloquial phrasing;
- self-deprecating jokes;
- playful frustration.

However, the humor is not random.

It is also a coping mechanism.

Shiboub hides fear and guilt behind comedy.

---

# 15. Shiboub’s Guilt

Shiboub carries a major secret.

Before the attack, he met the sorcerer who called himself عدو العرب and whom the Arabs would later name المُبدِّد.

The sorcerer claimed he had:

- no home;
- no shelter;
- nowhere safe to stay.

Shiboub trusted him.

Because of his own generosity, Shiboub allowed him into his home.

The sorcerer gradually:

- gained his trust;
- manipulated him;
- learned about Dar Al-Rawabit;
- convinced Shiboub to allow him access.

Shiboub unknowingly opened the way for the attack.

The villain exploited Shiboub’s kindness.

This event is the primary source of Shiboub’s guilt.

---

# 16. Shiboub’s Arc 1 Secret

Shiboub knows more about the villain than he admits.

During the first Chapters:

- he avoids certain questions;
- he becomes visibly uncomfortable around specific symbols;
- he recognizes traces of the villain;
- he changes the subject with humor.

His secret is fully confessed at the end of Chapter 4.

---

# 17. Overall Narrative Tone

## **Epic**

The central story should feel:

- large-scale;
- heroic;
- adventurous;
- culturally rooted;
- mysterious;
- emotionally meaningful.

Shiboub provides the comedic counterweight.

The intended balance is:

**Epic stakes + Mystery + Adventure + Shiboub comedy**

The game should not feel childish.

---

# 18. Story Presentation

The story uses:

## **Short animated story moments + dialogue bubbles**

Typical duration:

## **10–20 seconds**

Main Story Beats occur at:

- Chapter Start
- Chapter Midpoint
- Chapter Ending

Every Story Beat is skippable immediately.

Gameplay should never be held hostage by narrative.

---

# 19. Dialogue Language

Main story dialogue uses:

## **Simplified Modern Standard Arabic**

Shiboub may use:

- light colloquial expressions;
- widely understandable Arabic humor;
- short informal reactions.

His dialogue must not depend heavily on one regional dialect.

The goal is broad Arab-world accessibility.

---

# 20. Voice Acting — MVP

The MVP does not require full Arabic voice acting.

Approved approach:

- text dialogue;
- short vocal reactions;
- exclamations;
- laughter;
- surprise;
- sighs;
- brief character sounds.

Full Arabic dubbing can be evaluated later.

---

# 21. Story Replay

Unlocked story moments are replayable through a simple:

## **Story Archive**

The Story Archive is accessible from the Chapter interface.

It is not the same as a Lore Collectibles system.

Lore Collectibles are Post-MVP.

---

# 22. World Map

The game uses an artistic map centered on the Arab world.

Approved map style:

## **Parchment-inspired artistic map + magical glowing network + subtle 3D depth**

The map emphasizes:

- city Nodes;
- glowing connection lines;
- repaired areas;
- damaged/dark regions;
- network expansion.

It should not behave as a detailed political map.

The story focuses on places and connections, not geopolitical borders.

---

# 23. Chapter Rule

Each Chapter is located in a real city/place.

The place defines:

- visuals;
- local manifestation of Dar Al-Rawabit;
- ambience;
- music flavor;
- narrative events.

Gameplay Association content remains mixed.

A Cairo Chapter is not an “Egypt trivia chapter”.

A Marrakech Chapter is not a “Moroccan vocabulary chapter”.

---

# 24. Music Direction

The game has one recognizable core musical identity.

Every city adds:

- subtle regional instrumentation;
- local rhythmic flavor;
- environmental ambience.

The goal is continuity with cultural flavor.

Avoid caricature or stereotypical musical treatment.

## **صحوة الروابط — Awakening of Connections**

The first story arc contains five Chapters:

1. القاهرة: أول خيط

# 26. Chapter 1 — Cairo

## **القاهرة: أول خيط**

### Dar Al-Rawabit Manifestation

An old hidden library/khan behind a door in historic Cairo.

The interior is impossibly larger than the exterior.

It contains:

- endless shelves;
- suspended cards;
- glowing threads;
- old mechanisms;
- damaged chambers.

### Chapter Start

The opening sequence begins with one Arabic word written on a card.

The threads connecting it to other words begin to crack.

Then:

- more threads break;
- thousands of cards become disconnected;
- parts of Dar Al-Rawabit darken;
- the Distortion spreads.

Shiboub says:

> «الكلمات لسه موجودة… المشكلة إن الناس بدأت تنسى ليه معناها معنى.»

The player notices a connection thread that Shiboub cannot see.

This establishes بصيرة المعنى.

### Chapter Midpoint — APPROVED

The Distortion reacts directly to the player for the first time.

This confirms that it is conscious.

### Chapter Ending — APPROVED

The Cairo Node is repaired.

However, a broken line of light extends from Cairo toward Alexandria.

The player and Shiboub realize the damage has already spread.

---

# 27. Chapter 2 — Alexandria

## **الإسكندرية: أصداء الغياب**

### Dar Al-Rawabit Manifestation

A coastal archive beneath a fictional old lighthouse.

Parts of the archive are partially submerged.

Glowing connection threads can be seen moving beneath the water.

### Chapter Midpoint — APPROVED

The player discovers that the city archives are not separate.

They are all manifestations of one connected Dar Al-Rawabit network.

Damage in one Node can affect another.

### Chapter Ending — APPROVED

The player hears the voice of the sorcerer who calls himself عدو العرب for the first time.

He is not physically shown.

His presence becomes explicit.

Approved narrative direction for his first line:

> «لغة تحمل كل هذه المعاني... تستحق أن تُنسى.»

The exact final wording may be polished during script production without changing the meaning.

---

# 28. Chapter 3 — Beirut

## **بيروت: ما بين السطور**

### Dar Al-Rawabit Manifestation

A vertical house of books.

Its floors contain:

- suspended pages;
- memories floating in the air;
- fragments of sentences;
- unstable bridges made of text and light.

### Chapter Midpoint — APPROVED

The Distortion begins creating false relationships.

Words are not only losing meaning.

They are being deliberately attached to incorrect meanings.

This narratively introduces deeper semantic ambiguity.

### Chapter Ending — APPROVED

The player finds a symbol connected to عدو العرب / المُبدِّد.

Shiboub clearly recognizes it.

However, he hides the truth and pretends he does not know what it means.

The player begins to suspect that Shiboub is hiding something.

---

# 29. Chapter 4 — Marrakech

## **مراكش: متاهة المعنى**

### Dar Al-Rawabit Manifestation

A riad-like labyrinth of:

- corridors;
- courtyards;
- zellige-inspired surfaces;
- doors;
- rooms that rearrange themselves.

### Chapter Midpoint — APPROVED

The player discovers that The Distortion can reshape Dar Al-Rawabit itself.

The archive is no longer only losing connections.

Its physical/narrative structure can now be manipulated by the enemy.

### Chapter Ending — APPROVED

Shiboub finally confesses.

He tells the player:

- he knew the sorcerer;
- he hosted him;
- he trusted him;
- he allowed him access to Dar Al-Rawabit;
- he was manipulated.

This is Shiboub’s major emotional confession in Arc 1.

---

# 30. Chapter 5 — Dubai

## **دبي: ما بعد الذاكرة**

### Dar Al-Rawabit Manifestation

A futuristic elevated glass archive.

This proves that Dar Al-Rawabit is not merely ancient.

It evolves alongside humanity.

The archive contains:

- glass structures;
- luminous pathways;
- modern symbolic systems;
- data-like patterns mixed with traditional semantic threads.

### Chapter Midpoint — APPROVED

The player encounters عدو العرب — known among the Arabs as المُبدِّد — for the first time as a:

## projection / shadow manifestation

This is not a traditional boss fight.

The confrontation combines:

- dialogue;
- intimidation;
- a difficult puzzle sequence;
- stronger Distortion behavior.

The villain is still not fully physically exposed.

### Chapter Ending

The Dubai Node is repaired.

The Arc reaches its first major payoff.

---

# 31. Arc 1 Major Reveal

After repairing Dubai, the world map expands.

The player discovers:

## Dar Al-Rawabit itself is collapsing.

The problem was never limited to five cities.

The restored network reveals:

- hundreds of dark Nodes;
- damaged connection lines;
- regions far beyond the first Arc;
- some Nodes outside the Arab world.

These external Nodes exist because Arabic has historically interacted with:

- other languages;
- sciences;
- trade;
- literature;
- cultures;
- shared knowledge systems.

The threat is much larger than expected.

---

# 32. Arc 1 Final Moment

Shiboub looks at the expanded network.

The scale of the threat becomes clear.

Then he addresses the player for the first time as:

## **«يا أسطورة المعاني.»**

This is the canonical title moment.

The sequence may end with a title sting:

# **سوليتير العرب: أسطورة المعاني**

# 33. Arc 2 Direction

The next high-level story direction is:

## **The Arabian Peninsula**

Exact Arc 2 cities and Chapter order are intentionally left for the next narrative phase.

Arc 1 only needs to open the route toward this region.

---

# 34. Narrative Ending Policy

Every Arc must have:

- a satisfying local conclusion;
- meaningful character progression;
- at least one important reveal;
- a clear payoff.

However, the larger story continues.

This supports Endless progression without making each Arc feel unfinished.

---

# 35. Endless-Level Narrative Logic

Dar Al-Rawabit is alive.

As humans create:

- new meanings;
- new terms;
- new discoveries;
- new expressions;
- new cultural relationships;

the network grows.

Therefore:

- new Chapters can always exist;
- new relationships can always appear;
- the journey does not require a final Level.

---

# 36. LiveOps Narrative Integration

Temporary Events can appear as:

- temporary disturbances;
- unstable side Nodes;
- newly revealed archive branches;
- seasonal semantic anomalies.

Main Journey remains evergreen.

Events can contain:

- seasonal themes;
- regional culture;
- sports;
- current topics;
- dialect-focused content.

---

# 37. Post-MVP Narrative Systems

Deferred:

- Lore Collectibles;
- deep codex system;
- Permanent Special Packs;
- extended character histories;
- optional side stories;
- full voice acting;
- Leaderboard narrative framing.

---

# 38. Narrative Guardrails

The story must never:

- turn the game into anti-foreigner messaging;
- generalize the villain’s hatred to real-world peoples or nationalities;
- frame any real ethnicity/nationality as inherently hostile;
- require political interpretation;
- reduce the Main Journey to country trivia;
- force long cutscenes;
- block fast gameplay;
- contradict mixed Association content;
- make Shiboub’s comedy undermine major emotional scenes;
- reveal the hidden antagonist’s full truth too early.

عدو العرب is an individual fictional villain.

His hatred is his personal evil and must not be presented as representative of any real group.

---

# 39. Narrative Production Rules

All future narrative production must preserve:

- the official thesis;
- player’s Insight of Meaning;
- Shiboub’s guilt/backstory;
- عدو العرب as the main Arc-level villain;
- The Distortion as an intelligent awakened force;
- the five approved Arc 1 cities/order;
- Chapter subtitles;
- Node/network progression;
- gameplay-first story presentation.

Dialogue polishing is allowed.

Core meaning is not.
